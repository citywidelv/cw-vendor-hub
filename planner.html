Build a Starter Kit

Why a Starting Kit Matters

The fastest way to lose a new account is to show up short. One cloth and one mop head cannot clean a building, and the customer sees it on the first night. This page tells you what to have on the van before the first shift.

The one rule to remember

Keep one clean set for every day you clean. Wash once a week.

A set is the cloths and mop heads one cleaning uses. If you clean a building five nights a week, you need five sets. You use one each night, and on laundry day you wash all five.

If you clean only one day a week, keep two sets. One to use and one spare.

How to Count It

  1. Count what one cleaning uses. The table below does this for you, by building size.
  2. Multiply by the days you clean each week. Five nights means five times as many.
  3. Round cloths up to full packs. Cloths are sold in packs of 12.

Example. A medium office uses 5 green cloths a night. Cleaned 5 nights a week, that is 25 cloths. Buy 3 packs of 12.

Build Your List

1. What kind of building is it?
2. How big is it? Cleanable square feet.
3. How many days a week is it cleaned?
4. How will you mop? Pick one. You do not need both.

    The Color Code

    Each color has one job. A cloth that touched a toilet never touches a desk.

    Red or pink cloths. Toilets, urinals and the red zones in the restroom.
    Yellow cloths. Every other restroom surface. Sinks, counters, partitions, dispensers.
    Blue cloths. Glass and mirrors.
    Green cloths. General cleaning and dusting.
    Red or pink mop. Restroom floors. It never leaves the restroom.
    Blue or green mop. The floors in every other room.

    Fold each cloth in quarters. That gives you eight clean faces. Take a fresh cloth for every restroom and every new area. The full guide is on the Color Coding System page.

    Pick One Way to Mop

    Flat mop and charging bucket

    You soak clean pads in the bucket and use a fresh pad for each area. No wringing. Best for offices, clinics and any floor that has to look the same every night.

    A pad is used up after one restroom or a few rooms, so you carry more pads. They cost much less than a mop head.

    Loop mop and wringer bucket

    Also called a string or rag mop. Best for heavy soil, spills and big open floors like school halls and warehouses.

    Use a bucket with two sides, so the dirty water stays away from the clean water.

    Either way you need two colors. Red for restrooms and blue or green for everything else. You do not need both kinds of mop.

    What You Bring and What the Building Supplies

    You bring

    • Cloths and mops
    • Vacuum, buckets and tools
    • Chemicals
    • Gloves, safety glasses and wet floor signs
    • Liners for your own cart

    The building supplies

    • Toilet paper and paper towels
    • Hand soap
    • Liners for the building's trash cans

    You fill the dispensers from the building's own stock. If the stock runs low, tell City Wide on the Report Supplies Needed page.

    Chemicals

    The building decides the chemical program. Some buildings have a FastDraw or 3M cabinet on the closet wall. Some use EnvirOx. Some have no cabinet, and those use TruShot cartridges. Ask your FSM which one your building uses.

    The kit builder asks the same question and loads the right chemicals. Every kit also gets a toilet bowl cleaner, a scale remover and a stainless steel polish, sold one bottle at a time.

    Label every spray bottle. Never leave a bottle on site that does not say what is in it.

    Laundry Day

    • Dirty cloths and mop heads go in the hamper during the shift. Never back in the bucket or on the cart shelf.
    • Use one mesh wash bag for each color, so red never washes with blue.
    • Wash microfiber by itself. No bleach and no fabric softener. They ruin the fibers.
    • Dry on low heat.
    • Pick the day and the person. If nobody owns laundry day, you run out of clean cloths.

    Want to wash twice a week instead? Then you need about half as many sets.

    Where the Numbers Come From

    ISSA, the cleaning industry association, publishes how long cleaning tasks take. It does not publish a shopping list. So these counts come from trade practice and City Wide field experience. Treat them as a starting point. You know your building.

    Clinics, schools and food plants use half again as many cloths. Flat pads go up by half in clinics and schools and double in food plants.

    Before You Load the Van

    • The count matches the list above for this building, its size and its cleaning days
    • Every cloth color is stocked. Red, yellow, blue and green
    • One red mop and one blue or green mop for each cleaning day
    • Chemicals match the building's program and every spray bottle is labeled
    • Vacuum runs, the filter is clean, and spare bags are packed
    • Wet floor signs, gloves and safety glasses are in the vehicle
    • Laundry day is set. You know who washes and which day
    • Building keys, alarm codes and the scope of work are in hand
